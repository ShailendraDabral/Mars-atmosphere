In plain wordsA radio occultation profile lists temperature, pressure and air density at heights from the surface up to about 40 km. The synthetic generator builds profiles the same way the real retrieval does: pressure comes from hydrostatic balance and density from the ideal-gas law, so all three always agree.

Quality control and physics audit

Remove the unreliable top of each profile, then check that the reference data obey both physical laws.

In plain wordsEach profile is computed downward from a guessed temperature at the top, so the first few kilometres are uncertain. Levels whose stated uncertainty exceeds the threshold are dropped. The audit then recovers the molecular mass of Martian air that the retrieval assumed, and measures how well hydrostatic balance holds in the data. That last number is the floor: no method can be more hydrostatic than the data themselves.

Train, validation and test profiles

Whole profiles are assigned to one group, separately within each Mars year, so nothing from a test profile is ever used for training.

In plain wordsThe training profiles teach the models. The validation profiles are used to choose settings and to calibrate error bars. The test profiles stay untouched until the final comparison, like an exam the students never saw.

Gap scenarios

Hide a contiguous interval of a test profile. Gaps are measured in kilometres of geopotential height and placed at the bottom, middle or top.

In plain wordsA middle gap has data above and below it, so filling it is interpolation. A gap at the bottom or top has data on one side only, so filling it is extrapolation, which is much harder. Inside the gap no method sees any temperature, pressure or density; only the position (height, latitude, longitude, season) is known.

Learn from the training profiles

Fit the statistical prior (EOFs) and pre-train two identical neural networks: a data-only MLP and a physics-informed PINN. They differ only in the physics weight λ.

In plain wordsThe networks learn what Martian profiles typically look like from the training set. The PINN is also penalised whenever its output breaks hydrostatic balance, checked at random heights called collocation points. Several copies with different random starts form an ensemble whose spread estimates uncertainty.

Reconstruct one gap

Run any combination of methods on the gap chosen in step 4. Neural networks are fine-tuned on the observed part of this profile, pulled back toward their pre-trained weights (L2-SP, strength α).

In plain wordsSimple methods draw a line or curve across the gap. Hydrostatic integration fills temperature with a smooth curve and then computes pressure exactly from physics. The EOF method and the networks also use what they learned from other profiles. Compare the lines inside the yellow band with the black reference.

Benchmark (E1)

Every method on every test profile, gap size and location. Errors are averaged per profile, then compared profile by profile with Wilcoxon signed-rank tests and Holm correction.

In plain wordsOne lucky case proves nothing. The benchmark repeats the reconstruction many times and asks whether the differences between methods are larger than chance would produce. Brackets show 95 % confidence intervals.

Robustness experiments

Three further tests from the paper, on 10 km gaps.

E2 · Noisy observations

Noise of k × the retrieval uncertainty is added to the observed levels only; errors are measured against the clean reference.

E3 · Physics weight λ

The PINN is fine-tuned with different λ (no L2-SP pull) on middle and upper gaps. λ = 0 switches physics off.

E4 · Unseen Mars year

Retrain on two Mars years and test on the third. Each year covers a different season, so this is the hardest transfer.
